# Ruseward Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

Ruseward for Windows ("Ruseward") is made by an independent developer in Oman ("we"). It helps you check messages and links for scam warning signs, encrypt and decrypt private text, test passwords, and learn about scams.

## What Ruseward does with your data
- **Everything runs on your computer.** Messages, links, passwords and passphrases you type are processed in memory on your device and are not sent anywhere.
- **No account, no tracking.** Ruseward has no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting.
- **No internet connection is used.** The app does not contact any server on the internet.
- **Live quiz.** Only when you host a live quiz does Ruseward open a connection, and only on your local network: phones on the same Wi-Fi load the quiz page from your PC and send back the nickname the player types and their answers. These are held in memory on your PC, shown on the game screens, and deleted when the game ends or the app closes. Nothing about the game is sent to us or to the internet. Players should use a nickname rather than their full name.
- **Clipboard.** Ruseward reads the clipboard only when you press the "Paste from clipboard" button or one of its keyboard shortcuts (by default Ctrl+Alt+F8, Ctrl+Alt+F9 and Ctrl+Alt+F10). The text is processed in memory and not stored. If you turn it on, passphrases and decrypted messages you copy from Ruseward are wiped from the clipboard after the time you choose.
- **Your profile.** The first time Ruseward opens, it asks for a name and a colour, and optionally a PIN. This profile is stored only on your PC, in the app's own data folder. The PIN itself is never stored: only a one-way scrambled form of it (a salted PBKDF2 hash) that is used to check it. If you turn on Windows Hello, Windows checks your face, fingerprint or Windows PIN and only tells Ruseward yes or no. Ruseward never sees or stores your face or fingerprint. There is no online account, and "Forgot PIN" removes the profile and progress from your PC.
- **Recovery key, backups and moving to a new PC.** Ruseward can make a recovery key, which is shown to you once and not stored (only a check value is kept). Backups (.rwbackup files) are saved only where you choose, such as a folder on your PC, a USB drive or your own OneDrive folder. They are encrypted with your recovery key and cannot be opened without it. Ruseward does not upload them anywhere. When you send your profile to another PC, it goes directly over your own Wi-Fi, encrypted with a one-time code, and the sending stops after 10 minutes or once the new PC has it.
- **What is saved on your device.** Only your settings (shortcuts, theme, language, popup and clipboard options), simple counters and progress (how many messages you checked, scams you spotted, files you locked, which days you used the app for the safety streak, and badges earned), which quiz questions you have answered, and which steps you ticked in the "I think I was scammed" guide. These stay in the app's local storage on your computer. Uninstalling the app removes them.
- **Emails.** Email files you check, or email text you paste, are read in memory on your computer and are not saved or sent anywhere. Attachments are inspected as data only: Ruseward never opens, runs or saves them.
- **Pictures.** Screenshots and photos you check are read on your computer. QR codes are decoded by Ruseward itself, and text is read by the text recognition built into Windows. While text is being read, the picture is stored in a temporary file, which is deleted as soon as reading finishes. Pictures are never uploaded or kept.
- **Files and photos.** Files you lock or unlock are read and written only on your computer, and only where you choose to save them. Ruseward does not keep copies, and it never stores your passphrase.
- **Messages and passphrases are never saved.** Encrypted text is created only when you ask and is shown only on your screen.

## Sharing
Ruseward does not share, sell or transfer any data, because it does not collect any.

## Children
Ruseward does not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

The first time Ruseward opens, it asks how old the user is, so it can pick the right version: the Little version for children under 6, the kids version for ages 6 to 12, and the full app with a Teen zone for ages 13 to 17. The age, the nickname the child chooses, and the child's game progress (stars, stickers, lessons finished) are stored only on that PC. They are never sent to us or to anyone else, and there is nothing for us to delete because we never receive them. A parent can remove them at any time with "Forgot PIN" or by uninstalling the app.

In the kids version:
- The app asks for a nickname, not a real name.
- There are no ads, no purchases, no chat and no links out of the app.
- Features that send anything over the network are turned off: hosting a live quiz, moving a profile to another PC, and saving backups. The app also blocks them in its own background code, not only on screen.
- Messages a child checks with "Check with Wardy" are read in memory and not saved.
- A parent can add a grown-up PIN. After that, the PIN is needed to switch to the full app or change the PIN. It does not lock the child out of the kids version. If the grown-up forgets it, "Forgot the PIN?" starts over and removes the child's progress; on PCs with Windows Hello, that needs the Windows account owner to confirm first.
- In the Little version, Wardy reads text out loud from recordings that come with the app. Nothing is sent to any online speech service. If a sentence has no recording, a voice already installed in Windows is used, and only voices that run on the PC.
- The Teen zone (ages 13 to 17) is a page of guides and a practice quiz. Answers and scores stay on the PC.

## Limits of the checks
Scores and warnings are estimates based on common scam patterns. They are not a guarantee that a message or link is safe or unsafe.
